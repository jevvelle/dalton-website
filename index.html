EXPLANATION

Understanding color vision deficiency

Color vision deficiency happens when the light-sensing cone cells in the eye respond differently than typical, making some colors harder to tell apart. It's usually inherited and affects roughly 1 in 12 men and 1 in 200 women worldwide. Here are the three types Project Dalton simulates:

Protanopia

Missing or reduced red-sensing cones. Reds look darker and can be mistaken for black, brown, or green.

Deuteranopia

Missing or reduced green-sensing cones. Reds and greens both shift toward similar brownish-yellow tones.

Tritanopia

Missing or reduced blue-sensing cones, much rarer. Blues and yellows are the hardest pair to tell apart.

ABOUT

Bulacan State University

Project Dalton

Project Dalton is an AI-powered assistive tool that helps individuals with color vision deficiency recognize and understand colors for more inclusive daily interactions. It was built as a research and development project by Team Sepia at Bulacan State University.

Why "Dalton"?

The project is named after John Dalton, the scientist who published the first scientific paper on color blindness in 1798, after studying his own condition.
